Learning Objectives

What students learn in this lab

1. Disparate Impact & Parity

Formulate and compute Disparate Impact ratio (the EEOC 80% four-fifths rule) and Statistical Parity Difference across sensitive groups defined by protected attributes (gender, race, age).

2. Auditing Benchmark Data

Perform empirical audits on the COMPAS recidivism risk dataset and the US Census Adult Income dataset to uncover systemic historical sampling and label bias.

3. Pre-Processing Mitigation

Implement instance reweighting and disparate impact feature editing to balance privileged and unprivileged representation before model training.

4. Accuracy vs Fairness Trade-Off

Plot the Pareto frontier between classification accuracy (F1-score, AUC) and demographic fairness to evaluate decision trade-offs for compliance and executive governance.

Mathematical Formulations

Quantitative Fairness Metrics

Disparate Impact (DI)

The ratio of selection rates between unprivileged ($A=0$) and privileged ($A=1$) groups:

DI = P(Ŷ = 1 | A = 0) / P(Ŷ = 1 | A = 1)

Under regulatory guidelines (US EEOC), a value $DI < 0.80$ demonstrates adverse impact.

Statistical Parity Difference (SPD)

The absolute difference in positive outcome probability across protected groups:

SPD = P(Ŷ = 1 | A = 0) - P(Ŷ = 1 | A = 1)

A completely fair classifier under demographic parity satisfies $SPD = 0$.

Reweighting Weight Formulation

Instance weights assigned during pre-processing to eliminate empirical statistical dependence:

W(A=a, Y=y) = [P(A=a) × P(Y=y)] / P(A=a, Y=y)

Samples from under-represented positive pairs receive higher weights.

Faculty Teaching Prompts

Classroom Discussion & Reflection

During Class Activity

Ask students to compute the Disparate Impact ratio on the baseline logistic regression model. Then have them apply reweighting and observe what happens to overall accuracy versus false positive rate parity.

Governance & Policy Question

"Why might achieving demographic parity (equal selection rates) be legally required in hiring, but potentially counterproductive or unfair in medical diagnosis where true prevalence differs?"
