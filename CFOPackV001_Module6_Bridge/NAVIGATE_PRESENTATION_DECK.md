# SLIDE 2: THE PROBLEM

**Many CFOs Make Decisions By...**

- 🎲 **Gut feeling** → "This feels right"
- 🗣️ **HiPPO (Highest Paid Person's Opinion)** → "The CEO said so"
- 📊 **Partial analysis** → "Let me look at cost and pick cheapest"
- ⏱️ **Time pressure** → "We need to decide now"
- 🔄 **Incrementalism** → "Let's just try something"

**Result:** Decisions that don't stick. Wasted investment. Board confusion.

---

**The Better Way:**

**Use a systematic method.** Validate constraints. Quantify trade-offs. Document reasoning.

**That's what NAVIGATE does.**

**Speaker Notes:**
> "How many of you have seen a company make a 'strategic investment' that didn't deliver? Or fund a digital initiative that sat unused? Often it's not because the idea was bad — it's because the decision wasn't rigorous enough.
>
> NAVIGATE forces rigor. It makes your thinking transparent. And it's repeatable — once you learn it, you can apply it to any decision."

# SLIDE 3: WHAT IS NAVIGATE?

**An 8-Step Framework**

```
INPUT: Messy decision with competing options & constraints
  ↓
NAVIGATE (8 systematic steps)
  ↓
OUTPUT: Clear recommendation with evidence & roadmap
```

**The Steps:**

| Step | What | Why | Time |
|------|------|-----|------|
| **N** | **Note** constraints & context | You can't ignore reality | 15 min |
| **A** | **Analyze** baseline costs | Understand the pain | 15 min |
| **V** | **Validate** each option | Know what's possible | 20 min |
| **I** | **Inspect** outcomes | See the 90-day result | 20 min |
| **G** | **Gauge** trade-offs | Compare apples to apples | 20 min |
| **A** | **Assess** risks | Know what could go wrong | 20 min |
| **T** | **Take** decision | Make the call | 15 min |
| **E** | **Execute** plan | Show how you'll do it | 30 min |

**Total Time:** 2.5 hours to solve a complex decision.

---

**Speaker Notes:**
> "NAVIGATE is not complicated. It's systematic. Each step takes 15-30 minutes. By the time you reach 'Take' (step T), the decision is usually obvious.
>
> The power isn't in any single step. It's in doing all 8 in order. They build on each other."

---

# SLIDE 4: WHEN TO USE NAVIGATE

**NAVIGATE is for decisions that have:**

✅ **Multiple options** ("Which should we do?")  
✅ **Constraints** (budget, timeline, risk tolerance)  
✅ **Competing priorities** (cost vs. control, speed vs. quality)  
✅ **High stakes** (will affect business for years)  
✅ **Incomplete information** (you can't know the future)  

**Examples:**

- Which digital finance initiative to fund first?
- Should we upgrade our ERP or work around it?
- Do we buy or build this capability?
- How do we sequence a multi-year transformation?
- Which treasury decision reduces cash risk most?

**When NOT to use NAVIGATE:**

❌ Simple operational decisions ("Schedule the meeting")  
❌ Obvious choices ("Fire the person breaking rules")  
❌ Reversible low-risk decisions ("Try this experiment")

---

**Speaker Notes:**
> "NAVIGATE is for the decisions that keep you up at night. The ones where you genuinely don't know what to do, and you can't afford to get it wrong.
>
> If you're confident, don't use NAVIGATE. But if you're uncertain and the stakes are high — this is your method."

# SLIDE 6: STEP N — NOTE CONSTRAINTS

**What You Do:**

1. **Read the case/problem** — Get all the facts
2. **List what you know** — Company size, financials, structure
3. **List constraints** — Budget, timeline, Board expectations
4. **Identify stakeholders** — Who cares about this decision?

**Real Example (Asteron AP):**

```
WHAT WE KNOW:
- 7 legal entities
- 4 ERP systems
- AP takes 8 days
- 50,000 invoices/year

CONSTRAINTS:
- Budget: INR 14 crore
- Timeline: 90-day proof point
- Board wants visible ROI
```

**Why It Matters:**
If you ignore constraints, you'll recommend something that's impossible.

---

**Speaker Notes:**
> "This step is about grounding yourself in reality. You can't make a good decision if you're not clear on what's actually true and what the limits are.
>
> 'We need AP automation' is too vague. 'We have INR 14 crore and 90 days to show value' is specific."

---

# SLIDE 7: STEP A — ANALYZE BASELINE COSTS

**What You Do:**

1. **Identify the pain** — What's not working?
2. **Quantify the cost** — In money, time, or risk
3. **Calculate annual impact** — INR X crore per year
4. **This becomes your target** — How much value must your solution capture?

**Real Example (Asteron AP):**

```
PAIN POINT:          COST:
Manual matching      INR 0.4 cr/year (2 FTE)
Accrual errors       INR 0.3 cr/year (rework)
Compliance risk      INR 0.3 cr/year (audit)
Reconciliation lag   INR 0.2 cr/year (delay)
─────────────────────────────────────
TOTAL COST:          INR 1.2 cr/year
```

**Translation:** Any solution that saves 20% (INR 0.24 cr/year) pays for itself in ~5 years.

---

**Why It Matters:**
Without numbers, you can't compare options fairly. "This is expensive" means nothing. "It costs INR 1.2 crore/year in inefficiency" is actionable.

---

**Speaker Notes:**
> "This is where most CFOs skip ahead. They're eager to pick a solution. But STOP. Understand the baseline pain first.
>
> If AP inefficiency costs INR 1.2 crore/year, then an INR 14 crore solution makes sense only if it saves money AND improves control.
>
> If AP costs INR 0.2 crore/year, the same INR 14 crore solution is probably overkill."

---

# SLIDE 8: STEP V — VALIDATE OPTIONS

**What You Do:**

1. **List all options** — What are we considering?
2. **Extract promised benefits** — What does each claim to deliver?
3. **Estimate impact** — Can we quantify the benefit?
4. **Rate confidence** — How likely is this to work?

**Real Example (Asteron AP):**

| Option | Cost | Promised Benefit | Confidence |
|--------|------|------------------|-----------|
| Pure RPA | INR 12 cr | 80% auto-match | High |
| Redesign + RPA | INR 14 cr | 40% faster + zero gaps | High |
| Data Foundation | INR 22 cr | Clean data | Medium |
| ERP Upgrade | INR 35 cr | System + process fix | Medium |

---

**Why It Matters:**
You need to know what each option actually promises. Not what you think it promises — what the case/market/vendor says it delivers.

---

**Speaker Notes:**
> "At this point, all options look good on paper. Your job is to extract what they actually promise.
>
> Notice: Pure RPA is cheapest (INR 12 cr). Data Foundation is priciest (INR 22 cr). But cheapest ≠ best. You'll see why in the next step."

---

# SLIDE 9: STEP I — INSPECT 90-DAY OUTCOMES

**What You Do:**

1. **For each option, ask:** What happens at Week 4? Week 8? Week 12?
2. **What's the proof point?** What can you show the Board on Day 90?
3. **Is it visible?** Will executives see the impact?
4. **Is it sustainable?** Or does it fall apart after the proof point?

**Real Example (Asteron AP):**

```
PURE RPA:
Day 90 → 80% auto-match, but exceptions pile up
Visible? Yes (auto-match rate is obvious)
Risk? Exceptions become a new problem

REDESIGN + RPA:
Day 90 → 40% faster cycle, zero control gaps, 60% auto-match
Visible? Yes (timeline improvement is obvious)
Risk? Low (process is clean first)
```

---

**Why It Matters:**
Day 90 is when the Board decides whether to fund Year 2. If your solution doesn't deliver visible value by then, you're in trouble.

---

**Speaker Notes:**
> "The Board approved your budget. In 90 days, they'll ask: 'What did we get?'
>
> If you say 'We cleaned the data foundation, but benefits aren't visible yet,' they'll kill Year 2 funding.
>
> If you say 'We reduced AP cycle from 8 days to 4 days, and our audit found zero control gaps,' they'll fund the next phase."

---

# SLIDE 10: STEP G — GAUGE TRADE-OFFS

**What You Do:**

1. **Create a comparison matrix** — All options, all dimensions
2. **Score each** — High/Medium/Low or numerical
3. **Identify winners & losers** — Which option wins on what?
4. **Spot the tension** — What's the real trade-off?

**Real Example (Asteron AP):**

```
                Pure RPA  Redesign  Data Fdn  ERP
Cost             HIGH      MED       LOW     VERY LOW
Day 90 Value     HIGH      HIGH      LOW      LOW
Speed            HIGH      MED       LOW      LOW
Control Risk     MED       LOW       LOW      MEDIUM
Adoption         MED       MED       HIGH     HIGH
Enables Next?    NO        YES       YES      YES
```

**The Tension:**
- Pure RPA is fastest/cheapest but doesn't fix control or foundation
- Redesign + RPA is slower/more expensive but strengthens everything
- Data Foundation is the best long-term but visible value is delayed

---

**Why It Matters:**
This matrix is where the real decision lives. Once you see it, the winner is usually obvious.

---

**Speaker Notes:**
> "Look at this matrix. Every option wins at something.
>
> Your job isn't to find the perfect option (there isn't one). Your job is to decide: What matters MOST to our Board and our business?
>
> If the Board said 'Speed,' choose Pure RPA.  
> If the Board said 'Control,' choose Redesign + RPA.  
> If the Board said 'Foundation,' choose Data First.
>
> But you need to know the trade-offs. And this matrix shows them clearly."

---

# SLIDE 11: STEP A — ASSESS RISKS

**What You Do:**

1. **For your top option, list risks** — What could go wrong?
2. **Rate each** — Probability × Impact
3. **Suggest mitigations** — How do you reduce the risk?
4. **Confidence check** — After mitigation, do you still feel good?

**Real Example (Asteron AP — Redesign + RPA):**

| Risk | Probability | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|-------------|--------|-----------|
| Process redesign stalls | Medium | High | Involve AP team early |
| Automation breaks | Low | High | Phased rollout; UAT |
| Adoption resistance | Medium | Medium | Quick wins to build momentum |
| Timeline slip | Medium | Medium | External consultant; strict schedule |

---

**Why It Matters:**
Every decision has risks. The question is: Are they manageable?

---

**Speaker Notes:**
> "At this point, you've done the analysis. But before you make the call, ask: 'What could kill this?'
>
> If process redesign stalls, you're stuck. How do you prevent that? Involve the AP team so they own it.
>
> If automation breaks, users lose faith. How do you prevent that? Parallel run; phased rollout.
>
> After mitigation, are the risks acceptable? If yes — you're ready to decide."

---

# SLIDE 12: STEP T — TAKE DECISION

**What You Do:**

1. **State your recommendation** — "We recommend [Option]"
2. **Give 3 reasons** — Why this option over others
3. **Quantify impact** — What will it deliver?
4. **Set the sequencing** — What comes after this?

**Real Example (Asteron AP):**

> **RECOMMENDATION: Redesign + Selective RPA (INR 14 crore)**
>
> **Reason 1:** Strengthens control (not weakens it)  
> **Reason 2:** Delivers proof point in 90 days (Board will approve Year 2)  
> **Reason 3:** Enables scale (clean process = easier to automate further)
>
> **Impact:** AP cycle 8 → 4 days; zero control gaps; INR 0.5 cr annual savings
>
> **Sequencing:** After this proves successful → Close redesign (Month 4-6) → Data foundation (Month 7-12)

---

**Why It Matters:**
Your recommendation is only as good as your reasoning. "I think this is best" is not enough. Show the evidence.

---

**Speaker Notes:**
> "You've done all this work. Now make the call.
>
> Don't say 'Either RPA or Redesign could work.' Say 'We recommend Redesign because [reasons].'
>
> Decisiveness builds confidence. And you have the evidence to back it up."

---

# SLIDE 13: STEP E — EXECUTE PLAN

**What You Do:**

1. **Break into 3 phases** — 4 weeks each (12 weeks = 90 days)
2. **Define phase outcomes** — What must be complete in each?
3. **Assign owners** — Who's responsible for what?
4. **Track success metrics** — How do we measure?

**Real Example (Asteron AP):**

```
PHASE 1: FOUNDATION (Weeks 1-4)
- Streamline 3-way match rules
- Design digital approval workflow
- Identify exceptions by type
- Owner: AP Manager

PHASE 2: BUILD (Weeks 5-8)
- RPA configured for 60% of invoices
- Manual workflow for exceptions
- Training starts
- Owner: RPA Lead

PHASE 3: TEST & SCALE (Weeks 9-12)
- Full rollout to all 7 entities
- Measure cycle time & exception rate
- Final training & stabilization
- Owner: CFO & IT Lead

SUCCESS METRICS:
- Invoice cycle time: 4 days (from 8)
- Exception rate: <5/day
- Auto-match rate: 60%+
- Audit trail: 100% digital
```

---

**Why It Matters:**
A great recommendation means nothing if you can't execute it. This plan shows you've thought through the "how."

---

**Speaker Notes:**
> "The hardest part isn't deciding. It's doing.
>
> This 3-phase plan is your execution blueprint. Every phase has outcomes. Every outcome has an owner.
>
> When something goes wrong (and it will), you look at this plan and adjust. You don't panic — you have structure."

# APPENDIX: NAVIGATE vs. Other Methods

## How NAVIGATE Compares

### **vs. Spreadsheet Analysis**
- Spreadsheet: Shows numbers
- NAVIGATE: Shows thinking (why you chose those numbers)
- Winner: NAVIGATE (you understand the logic)

### **vs. McKinsey Framework**
- McKinsey: 4-box matrix, hypothesis-driven
- NAVIGATE: 8-step systematic process
- Winner: Both good; NAVIGATE is simpler

### **vs. Decision Matrix**
- Matrix: Weights different criteria
- NAVIGATE: Builds the matrix (and much more)
- Winner: NAVIGATE (matrix is Step G)

### **vs. Agile / Lean Startup**
- Agile: Try fast, iterate, learn
- NAVIGATE: Think first, then execute
- Winner: Both! Use NAVIGATE to decide what to build; use Agile to build it

## Worked Example: Treasury Decision

**Scenario:** Your company has INR 500 crore in cash. Rates are rising. Should you invest in short-term instruments or keep it in bank?

**NAVIGATE approach:**

**N:** Note constraints
- Cash needed for operations: INR 150 cr
- Available for investment: INR 350 cr
- Timeline: 90 days
- Board risk tolerance: Conservative

**A:** Analyze baseline
- Current earning: 6% on bank deposits = INR 21 cr/year
- Could earn 7.5% on T-bills = INR 26 cr/year
- Upside: INR 5 cr/year (but with some risk)

**V:** Validate options
- Keep in bank (safe, lower return)
- T-bills (slightly riskier, better return)
- Mixed (some of each)

**I:** Inspect 90-day outcomes
- Bank: INR 5.25 cr earned, 100% safe
- T-bills: INR 6.5 cr earned, very low risk
- Mixed: INR 5.75 cr earned, minimal risk

**G:** Gauge trade-offs
- Bank is safest but leaves INR 5 cr on the table
- T-bills earn more but have rate/liquidity risk
- Mixed balances both

**A:** Assess risks
- If rates drop, T-bills lose value (mitigation: ladder maturities)
- If cash needed early, T-bills have liquidity lag (mitigation: keep 25% in bank)

**T:** Take decision
- Recommend 75% T-bills + 25% bank
- Earn INR 24 cr (most of the upside)
- Keep liquidity (minimum risk)

**E:** Execute
- Month 1: Open T-bill account, move INR 262 cr
- Month 2: Stagger maturity dates
- Month 3: Monitor and rebalance quarterly

**Outcome:** +INR 3 cr annual vs. status quo, with minimal risk
