# Instructor Facilitation Guide: Module 6 Cases & Bridge

**How to teach case analysis using the 8-step method**

---

## Overview

This guide helps instructors facilitate case analysis for Module 6 Cases 1-6 using CFOPackV001's 8-step decision method.

**Target Audience:**
- MBA/MFin programs (finance strategy)
- Corporate finance training (CFOs, Controllers)
- Finance transformation workshops

**Time Options:**
- **Short (1 case, 2 hours):** Asteron AP or GenAI case + deep dive
- **Medium (3 cases, 6 hours):** Cases 1-3 + group discussions
- **Long (all 6 cases, 12-16 hours):** Full Module 6 + compare across all

---

## Before Class (Instructor Prep)

### **1. Read & Analyze Cases (2-3 hours)**

For EACH case, complete:
- [ ] All 8 steps (don't just read the case)
- [ ] Multiple recommendations (see what happens if you recommend Option A vs. B)
- [ ] Financial impact calculations (understand the numbers)

**Why?** You need to know:
- Which recommendation is easiest to defend
- Which is most surprising
- Where students typically get stuck
- What questions to ask to guide thinking

### **2. Prepare Example Analysis (1-2 hours)**

For the case you'll teach, create:
- [ ] Step-by-step working document (your thinking)
- [ ] Comparison matrix (all options side-by-side)
- [ ] Your recommendation + 3 reasons
- [ ] Anticipated student disagreements (and how to handle)

**Example:** If teaching Asteron AP (Case 1):
- Your recommendation: AP Automation + selective redesign (not pure automation)
- Alternative: Pure RPA (students might suggest this)
- How to guide: "What happens to controls if we automate the process as-is?"

### **3. Print & Annotate (30 min)**

- [ ] Print case materials (have hard copies)
- [ ] Print workbook Part 1 (show students the model)
- [ ] Annotate with teaching notes (where to pause, what to ask)

## Teaching Sequence

### **OPENING (15 minutes)**

**Goal:** Build context and energy

**Script:**

> "In CFOPackV001, you learned a decision-making method. Today, we're going to apply that method to real finance strategy decisions.
> 
> You work at a company facing a choice: Which digital finance initiative should we fund first? You have limited budget. You have competing priorities. What do you recommend?
> 
> This is the question every CFO faces. And we're going to solve it using data, not hunches."

**Key Points:**
- Connect to CFOPackV001 (method transfer)
- Make it real (this happens in companies)
- Show the decision is hard (not obvious)
- Promise they'll learn a repeatable approach

**Visual:** Show 3-4 different recommendations from different students. "These could all be right. Let's see."

---

### **METHOD REVIEW (20 minutes)**

**Goal:** Refresh the 8 steps

**Approach 1: Lecture (if class hasn't done CFOPackV001)**

> "The 8-step method is: Validate → Baseline → Analyze → Model → Trade-off → Risk → Decide → Execute.
> 
> Step 1 (Validate): What do we know? What's the constraint?  
> Step 2 (Baseline): How much does the problem cost?  
> Step 3 (Analyze): What does each option promise?  
> [Continue through Step 8]"

**Approach 2: Collaborative (if class did CFOPackV001)**

> "Who remembers Step 1? What did we do there?"  
> [Students: "We validated the data"]  
> "Right. We looked at what we had. Today, we'll validate the company's current state instead of data quality."

**Whiteboard the 8 steps.** Leave them up all class.

**Handout:** Quick reference card (they'll use it during case work)

---

### **CASE INTRODUCTION (15 minutes)**

**Goal:** Establish the business context

**Script (Example: Asteron AP Case):**

> "You're Vikram Sharma, CFO of Asteron Industrial India. Manufacturing company, INR 4,800 crore revenue. Your CFO asked you a hard question: 'Why does our AP process take 8 days when we have 50,000 invoices? Why does our team spend so much time on exceptions?'
> 
> You decided to look at the data. And you found that we're spending INR 1.2 crore/year on AP inefficiency.
> 
> Now you're standing in front of the Board investment committee. You have INR 14 crore to spend. What do you recommend?
> 
> There are 4 options: [List them]
> 
> Your job: Use the 8 steps to figure out which option is best."

**Key Points:**
- Establish the protagonist (make it personal)
- Quantify the problem (INR 1.2 cr pain)
- Set the constraint (INR 14 cr budget, 90-day proof point)
- Show the options are real (not toy problems)

**Visual:** Show a table of the 4 options + cost/benefit

---

### **WORKED EXAMPLE WALKTHROUGH (45 minutes)**

**Goal:** Model the thinking process

**Approach:** Walk through Part 1 of CASE_ANALYSIS_WORKBOOK.md step-by-step

**Step 1: Import & Validate (5 min)**

> "First, we list everything we know about Asteron. [Show table from workbook]
> 
> What do we know?
> - 7 legal entities
> - 4 ERP systems
> - AP takes 8 days
> - 41% of entries are manual
> 
> What's the constraint?
> - Budget: INR 28 crore
> - Timeline: 90-day proof point
> - Board wants value visible
> 
> Why does this matter? If we choose an option that takes 6 months to show value, the Board will kill it at Day 90."

**Pause for questions.** Then continue.

**Step 2: Baseline Forecast (5 min)**

> "How much does doing nothing cost?
> 
> [Show table: Pain → Annual Cost]
> 
> AP inefficiency costs INR 1.2 crore/year. So any option that saves 20% (INR 0.24 cr/year) pays for itself in roughly 5 years.
> 
> Wait, that doesn't sound good. But remember: The board wants a PROOF POINT, not a full payback. We show value in 90 days. The full payback is Year 2-3."

**Step 3: Collections Intelligence (5 min)**

> "Each option promises something different.
> 
> Pure RPA: 80% auto-match, but exceptions pile up
> Data Foundation: Clean data, but benefits not visible in 90 days  
> AP Redesign: Process is clean, then automate
> Predictive Planning: Future-looking, but depends on clean data
> 
> Which one moves the needle most? [Show in table]
> 
> AP Redesign + Selective Automation promises 40% faster cycle + 50% fewer exceptions. That's visible in 90 days. That's impressive to the Board."

**Step 4: Revised Forecast (5 min)**

> "But what actually happens if we choose that option?
> 
> Weeks 1-4: Redesign the process  
> Weeks 5-8: Build automation  
> Weeks 9-12: Test and measure  
> 
> Day 90: What can we show the Board? [Show deliverables]
> 
> This is our proof point. If the Board sees 40% faster cycle + zero control gaps, they'll approve Year 2."

**Step 5: Trade-off Matrix (5 min)**

> "Now we compare all options on one chart.
> 
> [Show matrix on whiteboard]
> 
> Pure RPA wins on cost (cheap) but loses on control (exceptions pile up).  
> Data Foundation wins on foundation but loses on speed (90-day visible value).  
> AP Redesign wins on control and proof point.  
> Planning loses because data quality is weak.
> 
> What's your initial reaction?"

**Pause. Take comments.**

**Step 6: Risk & Control (3 min)**

> "If we choose AP Redesign, what could go wrong?
> 
> [Discuss with class]
> 
> Adoption risk (people resist change), execution risk (it's complex), scope creep (we keep adding to the redesign).
> 
> How do we mitigate? Involve the AP team. Quick wins to build momentum. Clear timeline.
> 
> With mitigations, we feel good about this choice."

**Step 7: Decision Framework (5 min)**

> "So here's my recommendation: AP Redesign + Selective Automation.
> 
> Why? Three reasons:
> 1. Control gets stronger (not weaker)
> 2. Proof point is visible in 90 days (Board will approve)
> 3. This unlocks next initiatives (clean process = easier to scale automation)
> 
> This is the right balance of speed and safety."

**Show the memo.** Point out structure.

**Step 8: Operationalize (5 min)**

> "How do we execute?
> 
> [Show 3-phase roadmap]
> 
> Phase 1: Design  
> Phase 2: Build  
> Phase 3: Test  
> 
> Each phase has owners and success criteria. The Board knows exactly what to expect.
> 
> If Phase 1 slips, the Board knows Day 90 is at risk. That forces urgency."

### **GROUP EXERCISE (90 minutes)**

**Goal:** Let students do the analysis themselves

**Setup:**

**If 1 case (2 hours total):**
- Part 1 walkthrough (45 min)
- Group exercise (60 min)
- Discussion (15 min)

**If 3 cases (6 hours):**
- Day 1: Asteron AP case walkthrough (90 min) + groups do AP (90 min)
- Day 2: GenAI case walkthrough (45 min) + groups do GenAI + Vistara Tech (120 min)
- Day 3: CFO-CIO and Board Roadmap (120 min)

**Group Instructions:**

> "You're going to analyze a case.
> 
> Here's what you do:
> 
> 1. **Read the case** (15 min) — Everyone reads silently
> 
> 2. **Discuss (45 min)**:
>    - What do we know (Step 1)?
>    - What does the problem cost (Step 2)?
>    - What do the options promise (Step 3)?
>    - What's the 90-day outcome for each (Step 4)?
>    - Which wins on cost/speed/control (Step 5)?
>    - What risks concern you (Step 6)?
>    - What's your recommendation (Step 7)?
>    - How do we execute (Step 8)?
> 
> 3. **Build your memo** (30 min) — One person writes; everyone contributes
> 
> 4. **Prepare to present** (5 min) — Pick one person to explain your recommendation to the class
> 
> Use the workbook templates. Use the quick reference. Ask me questions."

**Facilitation During Group Work:**

**What to do:**
- [ ] Circulate between groups
- [ ] Listen to their reasoning (don't jump in)
- [ ] If they're stuck, ask a question (don't give answer)
- [ ] If they're arguing, validate ("Both are good questions")
- [ ] If they're off-track, gently redirect

**What NOT to do:**
- ❌ Don't give them your answer
- ❌ Don't correct them (let them see the logic)
- ❌ Don't rush them (thinking takes time)
- ❌ Don't make it feel like school ("You got it wrong")

**Questions to Ask:**

If stuck on Step 2: "How much does the problem cost annually?"  
If stuck on Step 5: "Which option moves the needle most?"  
If stuck on recommendation: "If you had to defend this choice to the Board, what would you say?"  
If disagreeing: "Why do you prefer Option A?"

---

### **GROUP PRESENTATIONS (30-45 minutes)**

**Goal:** Share thinking and compare approaches

**Format:**

Each group presents 5 minutes:
- Recommendation (1 min)
- 3 reasons why (2 min)
- Key risk + mitigation (1 min)
- Question for other groups (1 min)

**Facilitation:**

**While presenting:**
- [ ] Thank them
- [ ] Write recommendation on whiteboard
- [ ] Note key insights
- [ ] Ask clarifying questions

**After each:**
- [ ] Ask: "Did anyone recommend something different? Why?"
- [ ] Show the different memos from EXAMPLE_MEMOS_CASES_2_6.md
- [ ] "Both recommendations are defensible. Let's see what other CFOs chose..."

**Synthesis:**

> "Looking at your recommendations:
> 
> Group 1 chose Pure RPA (focus on speed)  
> Group 2 chose Redesign + Automation (focus on control)  
> Group 3 chose Data Foundation first (focus on foundation)
> 
> All three are reasonable given different priorities. In reality:
> 
> - If Asteron is a startup, maybe pure RPA is okay (growth > control)
> - If Asteron is highly regulated, redesign + automation makes sense
> - If this is part of a multi-year transformation, foundation makes sense
> 
> There's no single right answer. What matters is: Did you follow the method? Did you quantify trade-offs? Could you defend your choice?"

---

### **CLOSURE (15 minutes)**

**Goal:** Connect to big picture

**Script:**

> "You just solved a real CFO problem using a systematic method.
> 
> In your companies, you'll face:
> - Which digital finance initiative to fund first?
> - Should we upgrade our ERP?
> - Do we need a data foundation before analytics?
> - Should we hire AI talent or use outsourced vendors?
> 
> All of these use the same 8-step method you practiced today.
> 
> The method is: Validate constraints → Quantify pain → Analyze options → Model outcomes → Compare trade-offs → Assess risk → Make recommendation → Build roadmap.
> 
> When you face a big decision at work, use this method. It will guide you."

**Key Takeaways:**
- [ ] "The method works for any decision under constraint"
- [ ] "You must quantify the pain (not just talk about problems)"
- [ ] "Trade-offs are real (speed vs. control, cost vs. quality)"
- [ ] "Your recommendation is only as good as your reasoning"

**Optional:** Show how the cases connect to Module 6 labs
> "If you recommended AP automation, go to Lab 04 (RPA for Finance Operations) to learn how it's actually done. If you recommended data foundation, go to Lab 07 (Controls & Governance)."

## Common Student Mistakes & How to Handle

### **Mistake 1: "We should do everything"**

**What they say:** "We need AP automation AND a data foundation AND analytics."

**What's wrong:** Not realistic given budget constraint

**How to correct:** 

> "Great thinking. What if we could only do ONE in the next 90 days? Which creates the most credible proof point for the Board to fund Year 2?"

This forces prioritization.

---

### **Mistake 2: "We should choose the cheapest option"**

**What they say:** "Process redesign costs INR 9 crore, so let's do that instead of INR 12 crore for AP automation."

**What's wrong:** Cost isn't the only factor (control, speed, proof point matter)

**How to correct:**

> "Cost is one factor. What about the Board's timeline? They want to see value in 90 days. Can process redesign deliver that?"

This balances cost with other criteria.

---

### **Mistake 3: "The case doesn't say, so we can't decide"**

**What they say:** "The case doesn't tell us whether control is important, so we can't know which option to pick."

**What's wrong:** CFOs make decisions with incomplete information; they use judgment

**How to correct:**

> "You're right—the case is ambiguous. That's realistic. In real life, the Board will have said: 'Strengthen our control position.' Use that as context. What does that tell us about which option fits best?"

This teaches judgment under uncertainty.

---

### **Mistake 4: "Our recommendation is different from the example memo"**

**What they say:** "But the example memo recommends AP Redesign. We recommend Pure RPA."

**What's wrong:** Nothing—different recommendations can be valid

**How to correct:**

> "Both are defensible. The difference is: You prioritize cost and speed. The example prioritizes control and foundation-building. In reality, the CFO's priorities matter. What's important to YOUR Board?"

This validates multiple right answers.

---

### **Mistake 5: "We didn't finish the 8 steps"**

**What they say:** "We got to Step 5 (trade-off matrix), but didn't write the recommendation."

**What's wrong:** The memo is the deliverable; thinking alone isn't enough

**How to correct:**

> "Okay. Your trade-off matrix is good. Now, write 3 sentences: What's your recommendation, and why? That's the memo."

Push them to complete the analysis.

## Assessment

### **Group Work (60% of grade)**

- [ ] Did they complete all 8 steps?
- [ ] Is the analysis quantified (numbers, not opinions)?
- [ ] Is the recommendation defensible?
- [ ] Is the memo clear and professional?

**Grading Scale:**
- 90-100: All 8 steps clear, recommendation well-reasoned, memo professional
- 80-89: All 8 steps present, some unclear, recommendation reasonable
- 70-79: Most steps present, analysis surface-level, recommendation weak
- <70: Incomplete or unclear analysis

---

### **Class Participation (40% of grade)**

- [ ] Did they engage in group discussions?
- [ ] Did they ask good questions during walkthrough?
- [ ] Did they contribute to presentations?
- [ ] Did they listen and respect other groups' recommendations?

## Tips for Facilitators

### **Tip 1: Be Comfortable with Ambiguity**

The cases don't have one "right answer." That's intentional. CFO decisions are often ambiguous. Your job is to help students make **clear, defensible choices with incomplete information**.

### **Tip 2: Listen More Than You Speak**

During group work, listen to their reasoning. Ask questions instead of giving answers. This teaches them to think, not to memorize your thinking.

### **Tip 3: Validate All Recommendations**

Even if a group chose Pure RPA (which you think is wrong), find something to validate:
> "I like how you prioritized cost and speed. If Asteron had said 'We need the cheapest option,' you'd be right."

Then introduce the trade-off:
> "But notice the control risk. How would you mitigate that?"

### **Tip 4: Use the Memo as the Deliverable**

Don't accept "Here's our thinking" without a memo. The memo forces clarity. Groups that struggle to write a one-page memo usually have unclear thinking.

### **Tip 5: Celebrate Good Questions**

When a student asks: "What if our assumption about adoption is wrong?" or "How does this affect the Board timeline?" — celebrate it.

> "That's the right question. You're thinking like a CFO."

## Troubleshooting

### **Problem: Group is arguing about recommendation**

**What to do:**
- Don't referee
- Ask: "What assumption are you disagreeing on?"
- Usually the disagreement is about risk tolerance or priority
- Say: "Both are valid depending on the Board's priorities. Document both views in your memo."

### **Problem: Group finished early**

**What to do:**
- Don't let them leave
- Ask: "What risks didn't you explore?" or "How does this compare to the example memo?"
- Push them to depth: "Why is Step 6 (risk) important here?"

### **Problem: Group is stuck on Step 2**

**What to do:**
- This is the most important step
- Don't move them forward until they have numbers
- Ask: "What's the cost of the problem? Find a number in the case."
- If no number: "Estimate it. What would you guess?"

### **Problem: Group's recommendation is very different from your expectation**

**What to do:**
- Don't say "That's wrong"
- Ask: "Talk me through your logic"
- Look for the valid reasoning underneath
- If it's sound, celebrate it ("That's a different but defensible choice")
- If it's flawed, ask guiding questions

### **Problem: Presentation is unclear**

**What to do:**
- Thank them
- Ask the class: "Did you understand why they made that choice?"
- If no: Ask the presenting group: "Can you explain Step 5 (trade-off matrix) one more time?"
- Clarity matters more than being right

## Final Thoughts

Your role as an instructor is to:
1. **Model the method** (show them how to think)
2. **Challenge their thinking** (ask good questions)
3. **Validate multiple approaches** (there's rarely one right answer)
4. **Build confidence** (help them see they can solve complex decisions)

If students leave the class believing "I can use this method on real problems at work," you've succeeded.
