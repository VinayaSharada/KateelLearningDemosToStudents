What this workshop is about

The problem first, the notebooks second

Your baseline forecast shows healthy cash in 14 days — but it assumes every invoice pays exactly on time. Historical data says otherwise. By the end, you'll know the real gap and what to do about it.

Learning objectives

  • Identify a cash gap by comparing an optimistic baseline forecast to an ML-adjusted realistic forecast.
  • Train and interpret a payment-timing prediction model on historical invoice data.
  • Model and compare working-capital levers by cash impact, timeline, and feasibility.
  • Write a CFO-ready decision memo and a phased implementation plan.

Run modes

  • Supported modes: local Jupyter, or upload the .py notebooks to Colab
  • Demo type: sequential notebook workshop (not a single-page interactive demo)
  • Primary launch surface: index.html (notebook + guide hub)

Before you start

Needs Python 3.8+ and Jupyter (or Colab). No API key required — Claude/Groq integration is optional, for interpreting results.

Read participant/GETTING_STARTED.md at least a day before your session.

Business context

Why this workshop matters

This mirrors a real treasury workflow: reconcile data, forecast, predict, decide, execute — with 10,000 synthetic invoices ($546.9M AR, 148 customers) standing in for a real book of business.

Core scenario

Baseline forecast: $2.3M on Day 14. Realistic forecast once late-payment behavior is modeled: $1.8M. That $500K gap is what the workshop is built to close.

Concepts covered

●Cash flow forecasting
●ML collections prediction
●Working capital levers
●FX hedge policy
●CFO decision memos
●Implementation planning

What participants should note

Baseline assumptions are optimistic by design — the workshop's core lesson is that realistic payment-timing data changes the decision, not just the number.

How to run the workshop

Recommended flow

Sequence

  • Optional: N0.5 bank reconciliation (30-40 min) if you want to demonstrate data prep first.
  • N1 Import & Validate → N2 Baseline Forecast → N3 Collections ML → N4 Revised Forecast.
  • ☕ Break, then N5 Working Capital Levers → N6 FX Hedge → N7 Decision Memo → N8 Operationalize.

Team setup

  • Teams of 2-3, rotating three roles: Treasurer/Operator, Controller/Risk, CFO Spokesperson.
  • Roles rotate at the halfway point so everyone gets time in each seat.

Optional Claude/LLM help

  • Copy-paste prompts in claude_prompts/ for data prep, result interpretation, and memo polish — entirely optional.

Limitations

What's illustrative, and what stays a real decision

Data note

All data is fully synthetic (10,000 invoices, 148 customers, 24 months of payment history) — realistic in shape, not a real company's books.

Human judgment point

The generated memo and plan are a starting draft. A real CFO decision still needs your own risk tolerance, supplier relationships, and approval authority applied on top.

Safe reuse

Replace the synthetic CSVs with your own anonymized company data (see participant/DATA_UPLOAD_GUIDE.md) and rerun N1-N8 for a real analysis.

Self-service reuse

Extend it yourself, then adapt it safely

What you can enhance on your own

  • Swap in your own invoices/payments/customers CSVs and rerun the pipeline.
  • Adjust the ML model parameters in N3, or the forecast horizon in N2/N4.
  • Add a new working-capital lever to N5 beyond collections/payables/inventory.

How to adapt this to your use case

  • Anonymize real company data before use (see data/README.md).
  • Customize templates/ (decision memo, governance framework) with your company's approval chain.
  • Keep the memo and plan advisory until your actual CFO/Controller signs off.

Audience fit

Built for CFO workshops, treasury teams, and executive-education participants — no coding background required, just willingness to run notebooks and read output.

Faculty guide

Prompt for discussion or assessment

Ask each team to defend their working-capital lever choice in a 5-minute CFO pitch: what's the impact, the timeline, and the biggest risk if it doesn't work?
