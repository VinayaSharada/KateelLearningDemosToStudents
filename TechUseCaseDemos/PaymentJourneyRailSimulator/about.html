What this demo is about

Map the rail before judging the product

Students choose a payment rail and compare who participates, where the customer sees success, where institutions get finality, what reversals look like, and who carries liquidity or fraud burden.

Learning objectives

  • Compare payment rails across participants, message flow, and settlement structure.
  • Separate customer experience from legal or operational finality.
  • Explain how fraud, fees, and liquidity implications change by rail.

Best fit

Use this first in the course. It gives the mental map needed for UPI, cards, wallets, reconciliation, CBDC, and programmable-money sessions.

Teacher cue

Ask students where the payment looks complete to the customer, where it becomes financially settled for institutions, and where a later exception can still reopen work.

Self-service reuse

Extend the map, then localize the assumptions

What you can enhance on your own

  • Add more rails or cross-border corridor variants.
  • Add explicit export or comparison summaries by rail.
  • Add stronger separation between customer success, settlement, and legal finality.

How to adapt this demo to your use case

  • Replace the sample timing and fee assumptions with local scheme realities.
  • Revalidate what finality means in your own product, bank, or market context.
  • Keep the core rail logic stable and shift course framing into the facilitator narrative.

Audience fit

This demo works for payments, banking, treasury, fintech-product, risk, and teaching audiences because it provides a neutral system map before any one stakeholder starts optimizing their own piece.
