What this demo is about

CBDC design is a policy trade-off engine, not a single best answer

The studio helps learners compare how scope, representation, architecture, privacy, limits, offline capability, and programmability interact when a jurisdiction designs a CBDC.

Learning objectives

  • Compare retail and wholesale CBDC objectives.
  • Explain how privacy, control, inclusion, and resilience move together.
  • Discuss why CBDC design remains a policy and governance choice, not only a technology choice.

Best fit

Use this after students understand payment rails and digital-money basics, when the discussion shifts from transaction flow to system design.

Teacher cue

Ask students which stakeholder gains from each design choice and which stakeholder bears the cost or control burden.

Self-service reuse

Extend the policy scorecard, then localize the institutional constraints

What you can enhance on your own

  • Add policy presets, stakeholder scorecards, and side-by-side design comparison exports.
  • Add more visible trade-offs around privacy, bank disintermediation, and offline resilience.
  • Add jurisdiction-specific design dimensions when needed.

How to adapt this demo to your use case

  • Replace the sample assumptions with the policy questions and legal priorities relevant to your context.
  • Separate descriptive design choices from contested normative judgments before executive use.
  • Keep the simulator neutral and place recommendations in surrounding teaching or policy material.

Audience fit

This demo can support payments, public-policy, banking, treasury, and classroom audiences because it frames CBDC design as a structured choice problem rather than a one-sided technology pitch.
