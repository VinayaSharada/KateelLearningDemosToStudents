PII Detector

A browser-based demonstration of personally identifiable information detection and the compliance implications of automated scanning.

What This Demo Is About

Every day, compliance teams face a critical challenge: How do we know what sensitive data is exposed in our documents?

Before sharing a contract with a regulator, publishing a case study, or uploading an employee handbook to the cloud, organizations must audit the document for personally identifiable information (PII). The problem is scale: with thousands of documents and limited staff, automated detection becomes necessary.

This demo shows how automated PII detection works in practice. Students will:

  • Scan real documents (contracts, policies, applications) for sensitive data
  • See what the detector finds and with what confidence
  • Discover its limitations: false positives, missed entities, context sensitivity
  • Learn why human review is always required before acting on results

The Business Context

Compliance Risk: The Cost of Exposure

Exposing PII carries severe consequences:

  • GDPR Fines: Up to €20 million or 4% of global revenue
  • CCPA Penalties: Up to $7,500 per intentional violation
  • Reputational Damage: Loss of customer trust and brand value
  • Breach Notification: Mandatory disclosure, legal liability, forensics costs

The Detection Problem

Manual review doesn't scale:

  • A single employee handbook might contain dozens of names, emails, phone numbers
  • Customer contract archives span thousands of documents
  • Legacy systems may have lost track of what data exists
  • Staff can't read every page of every document

Solution: Automated scanning finds PII at scale. But automated tools make mistakes—false positives waste review time, and false negatives miss real risk.

How the Demo Works

Input Methods

  • Paste Text: Copy-paste any text into the demo (contracts, policies, emails, etc.)
  • Upload PDF: Select a PDF file (up to 10 MB). Text is extracted automatically.
  • Sample Documents: Load realistic examples: loan applications, employment contracts, privacy policies, invoices

Detection Process

The detector uses pattern matching to identify PII types:

  • Email Addresses: Regular expression for format validation
  • Phone Numbers: Pattern matching for US phone format (extensible)
  • Social Security Numbers: 123-45-6789 format with validation
  • Credit Card Numbers: Luhn algorithm validation
  • Street Addresses: Address pattern with city, state, ZIP
  • ZIP Codes: 5-digit or 5+4 format

Confidence Scoring

Each entity gets a confidence score (0-100%). Higher scores indicate higher likelihood of real PII:

  • 95%+: Very likely (e.g., SSN matches validation rules)
  • 80-95%: Likely (e.g., email format + domain)
  • Below 80%: Possible but uncertain (context-dependent)

Output

  • Highlighted Text: PII marked in the original document
  • Summary Stats: Total entities, types found, average confidence
  • Entity List: Each PII item with type, confidence, and exact text

Key Learning Objectives

1. Understanding PII

Question: What counts as PII?

Learning: PII is any information that identifies or can identify an individual. Context matters:

  • A name alone is not PII (too common)
  • But a name + address is PII
  • An email address is PII (can identify sender)
  • A phone number is PII (can contact or track someone)
  • A ZIP code might be PII in combination with other data (quasi-identifiers)

2. The Limits of Automated Detection

Question: Can we automate PII detection completely?

Learning: Automation catches obvious patterns but fails on context:

  • False Positives: "123 Main Street" might be a sample address, not real data
  • False Negatives: A first name alone ("John") isn't detected, but "John + last name + email" is clearly one person
  • Context Sensitivity: "John Smith" in a case study vs. in a contact list has different risk profiles
  • Disguised PII: "SSN ending in 1234" is harder to detect than full SSN
  • Encoded Data: Hashed or encrypted PII looks like random text

3. Why Human Review Is Required

Question: Should we trust automated results?

Learning: No. Compliance teams must always review before acting:

  • Confirm Context: Is "555-1234" a real phone number or part of an example?
  • Assess Risk: Is this SSN in a public document or internal-only?
  • Understand Sensitivity: A customer name in a service log vs. a customer name in a public testimonial
  • Prioritize Remediation: What exposure is highest priority? Which documents matter most?

4. Governance & Workflow

Question: How should we operationalize PII detection?

Learning: A robust workflow includes:

  • Scanning Phase: Automated detection at scale
  • Triage Phase: Human review of high-risk findings
  • Remediation Phase: Redaction, deletion, or relocation of sensitive data
  • Verification Phase: Confirm remediation is complete and correct
  • Audit Trail: Document what was found, who reviewed it, what was done

Socratic Questions for Discussion

  • Q1: Your detector found 47 entities in a customer contract. When should you start worrying, and when should you dismiss a finding?
  • Q2: If the detector has 85% confidence, is that high enough to redact data without human review?
  • Q3: A sample PDF contains "555-1234" (a classic fake number). Will the detector flag it? Should it?
  • Q4: You find a customer's full SSN in an old email. Who should be notified, and what's your next step?
  • Q5: Your company publishes customer success stories with names and job titles. Is that PII? Should the detector flag it?

Real-World Applications

  • Pre-Publication Audit: Before releasing case studies, white papers, or testimonials
  • Third-Party Risk: Scanning documents shared with vendors, regulators, or partners
  • Data Inventory: Identifying where PII exists in legacy systems or archives
  • Breach Response: Quickly assessing what data was exposed in a security incident
  • Compliance Audit: Evidence that your organization scans and reviews documents before sharing

Limitations & Considerations

  • Browser-Based Only: This demo runs entirely in your browser. No data is sent to a server.
  • Pattern Matching: Uses regex and pattern rules, not machine learning. More transparent but less sophisticated.
  • English Text: Optimized for English. Non-English PII patterns may not work as well.
  • PDF Limits: Complex PDFs (images, scanned documents, encrypted files) may not extract text correctly.
  • Performance: Large documents (100+ pages) may take time to process.
  • No Persistence: Results are not saved. Refresh to clear all data.

For Teachers

Assignment Ideas

  • Audit Task: Students receive a document and must use the detector to identify PII. Then they manually review and explain why the detector succeeded or failed.
  • Policy Analysis: Students scan their own company's public documents (website, blog, case studies) to see what PII is exposed. What should be redacted?
  • Risk Assessment: Given a set of detected PII, students must prioritize remediation: which exposures are highest risk? Why?
  • Workflow Design: Students design a PII detection and remediation workflow for their organization. How would they scale this?

Discussion Prompts

  • Why do compliance regulations mandate PII detection? (GDPR, CCPA, industry-specific rules)
  • What's the cost of over-detecting vs. under-detecting PII?
  • How would you explain automated detection confidence to a non-technical stakeholder?
  • What role does context play in PII detection? Can a machine ever fully replace human judgment?
