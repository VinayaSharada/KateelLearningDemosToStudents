About: Supply-Chain Disruption War Room

Concept, learning objectives and how to adapt this demo to your data

Overview

Two disruptions land the same morning: container congestion at a major port (+21 days on sea freight) and a fire at the primary casting supplier (4 weeks down). A war-room agent swarm — Supply Chain, Procurement, Finance, Comms — turns nine ordinary planning exports into line-stop dates, a priced mitigation plan and a customer-contact list, then stops at the approvals. Fictional company (Kaveri Auto Components Ltd, INR); all data synthetic.

The architecture idea (the part worth stealing)

Deterministic core, agentic shell. The scenario engine, the 13-week stock projection, the line-stop math and every mitigation price are plain auditable code. Agents interpret, argue execution risk and draft communications — they never produce a figure, and they never execute: the ₹-crore expedite, the customer note and the emergency supplier qualification all wait for named human signatures.

Learning objectives

  • Scenario latency is a strategic asset: "what if it gets worse?" goes from a three-day analyst exercise to an instant re-derivation — compare the four scenarios on the launch page.
  • Price the levers, then decide: air uplift vs alternate supplier vs accept, each with a cost and a relief date — including the honest case where no economic fix exists.
  • The swarm mirrors the org chart: each agent is a function at the table; the orchestrator is the meeting you no longer schedule.

The business decision it supports

Spend ₹0.61 cr today to protect ₹1.71 cr of margin — or wait for certainty and own the full ₹3.11 cr exposure?

Run modes

Browser (this page): four pre-computed scenarios, no setup, no keys.
Live tier (registered participants): the full Python scenario engine with shock sliders (port delay 0–35 days, outage 0–8 weeks), a Streamlit war room, and the multi-provider agent swarm (Anthropic / OpenAI / OpenRouter / Groq via a .env file).

How to adapt this demo to your use case

The live tier validates nine CSV contracts — products, BOM, components, suppliers, component-supplier options, inventory, inbound shipments, key accounts, freight rates — the shapes your MRP already exports. Add your own ports, single-source suppliers and monsoon weeks to a scenarios file, and run your real top-5 risks as a quarterly business-continuity drill.
