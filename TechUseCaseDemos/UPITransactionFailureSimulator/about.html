What this demo is about

UPI is fast, but fast does not mean simple

This demo focuses on the operational state machine around UPI: what happens when one participant thinks the payment is done and another still sees it as pending, duplicated, or under review.

Learning objectives

  • Compare push and collect flow responsibilities.
  • Explain why pending and timeout states create support and reconciliation costs.
  • Connect fraud controls and limits to customer-friction trade-offs.

Best fit

Use this after the rail simulator, once students already know the major UPI participants and now need to see where operational exceptions appear.

Teacher cue

Ask whether the right KPI is raw speed, low fraud, low complaints, or low unresolved pendings. Good payment operations usually require trading off all four.

Self-service reuse

Extend the failure tree before you operationalize the lesson

What you can enhance on your own

  • Add more pending-state branches, duplicate-retry patterns, and reversal outcomes.
  • Add complaint, support-load, or unresolved-pending metrics.
  • Add a reconciliation-file or reference-matching view for operations teaching.

How to adapt this demo to your use case

  • Replace the sample limit and fraud-review assumptions with your own operating context.
  • Decide which exceptions are customer-visible and which remain back-office-only.
  • Keep the state machine transparent unless you later connect it to live operational data.

Audience fit

This demo supports payments operations, fraud teams, customer-support training, product discussions, and faculty-led classes because it makes exception handling visible rather than abstract.
