Learning outcomes

What this course pack builds

Core outcomes

  • Explain digital payment journeys across UPI, cards, wallets, bank transfers, and tokenized payment designs.
  • Compare authorization, clearing, settlement, reversals, fraud controls, and reconciliation responsibilities by payment mode.
  • Translate payment-system mechanics into customer-experience, liquidity, economics, and operational-risk decisions.
  • Distinguish payment hype from operational reality when evaluating CBDC, stablecoin, and programmable-money use cases.

Audience fit

payment rails, UPI, cards, reconciliation, CBDC, wallet operations

Every card leads to an About page first, then to the launch surface. Run-mode and readiness badges are shown directly on the card.

Payment Journey and Rail Simulator

All Levels

Choose UPI, cards, wallets, NEFT, RTGS, cash, or Bitcoin and watch the message flow, participants, clearing and settlement path, time to finality, fees, liquidity implications, and failure handling change in real time. This is the course’s anchor system-map demo.

Teacher cue: Ask: "At which step does the customer think the payment is done, and at which step is the institution actually safe?" This separates experience from operational finality.

UPI Transaction and Failure Simulator

Intermediate

Simulate payer PSP, payee PSP, NPCI switch, remitter bank, and beneficiary bank across push and collect flows. Compare timeouts, duplicates, reversals, fraud checks, limits, and reconciliation exceptions without hiding the operational state machine.

Teacher cue: Ask: "Why is ‘pending’ often the most dangerous state in instant payments?" This reveals where customer, fraud, and reconciliation pain collide.

Card Network Economics and Settlement Simulator

Intermediate

Follow the cardholder → merchant → acquirer → network → issuer chain and see how interchange, MDR, gateway costs, fraud loss, refunds, chargebacks, and settlement delay determine merchant margin and issuer economics.

Teacher cue: Push on unit economics: "If gross sales rise but chargebacks and fees rise faster, did the merchant really win?"

Payment Operations and Reconciliation Workbench

Intermediate to Advanced

Work through bank, gateway, network, and merchant records with deliberate mismatches. Classify missing transactions, duplicates, fee discrepancies, pending reversals, chargeback timing, and settlement-date differences.

Teacher cue: Ask: "Which exception actually changes money owed, and which one only changes timing or reporting?" This teaches operational materiality.

CBDC Design Studio

Advanced

Configure retail vs wholesale, token vs account, direct vs hybrid, privacy, offline support, holding limits, interest, and programmability. See how these design choices reallocate resilience, policy control, inclusion, and bank-disintermediation risk.

Teacher cue: Ask: "Which CBDC choice solves one policy problem by creating another operational problem?" That keeps the discussion grounded.
